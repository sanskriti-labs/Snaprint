A xerox shop is one of the most boring businesses to talk about -- and one of the most reliable to run. Every three to four months, demand rolls back in: admission season, exam season, government form season, Aadhaar reprints, rental agreement prints, college project submissions. The rhythm is so consistent that running a xerox shop in India still looks like a viable small business in 2026, even after two decades of "digital India" headlines. This business plan lays out the realistic numbers -- what to set up, what it costs, what you actually earn, and when the money comes back.

## Why a xerox shop is still a good business in 2026

There's a paradox that anyone running a print shop already understands: India's going increasingly digital, and printing demand is still going up. The reason is straightforward -- the act of submitting a document, applying for a service, or signing a rental agreement still requires a physical paper copy. The person filling the form is online; the form itself is not.

Demand drivers that are still active in 2026:

- **College and exam seasons** -- every semester-end brings a wave of project prints, binding jobs, and last-minute question-paper runs. A well-placed shop near a college gate can see 3–5x volume spikes around exams.
- **Government forms and ID reprints** -- Aadhaar updates, PAN corrections, passport applications, driving licence renewals. These don't go away.
- **Rental agreements and legal documents** -- paper, stamp paper, notarised copies. Tenants and landlords print these every day.
- **Stationery and value-add services** -- the same counter is also selling pens, notebooks, and increasingly doing lamination, spiral binding, and ID photo printing.

The market never gets exciting, but it never collapses either. That's the actual appeal.

## Revenue model

Per-page economics in a typical Bengaluru / Tier 1 city shop in 2026:

| Service | Rate charged | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| B&W print (single side) | ₹1–₹2 | Highest volume line; margin ~50–60% |
| B&W print (double side) | ₹2–₹4 | Most common print job |
| Color print (single side) | ₹5–₹10 | Lower volume, higher margin |
| Color print (double side) | ₹10–₹20 | Project prints, charts |
| Spiral binding | ₹30–₹100 | High add-on margin |
| Comb binding | ₹20–₹40 | Bulk submission work |
| Lamination (A4) | ₹20–₹50 | ID, certificate, document |
| Passport photos (4 photos) | ₹60–₹100 | Steady add-on |

Volume estimates by area type:

| Location type | Daily B&W pages | Daily color pages | Bindings/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near college gate | 800–2,000 | 50–150 | 10–25 |
| Commercial / office cluster | 600–1,200 | 30–80 | 3–8 |
| Residential neighbourhood | 200–500 | 10–25 | 1–3 |
| Standalone remote / low traffic | 50–150 | 0–5 | 0–1 |

Revenue per page is small. Volume is everything. A college-area shop doing 1,000 B&W pages/day at an average ₹2.50 per page earns roughly ₹2,500/day from B&W alone -- before color, binding, and add-ons.

## Breakeven timeline

For the mid-traffic shop with the ₹4–5L capex:

- **Months 1–3:** Loss-making. You are still building a customer base, learning the rates the street will pay, and stabilising supply of paper / toner.
- **Months 4–8:** Approaching break-even. Monthly revenue is climbing, but you're still recovering the deposit.
- **Months 9–14:** Operating cash flow positive. You're paying yourself a modest salary and have headroom.
- **Months 14–22:** Full capital recovered. The shop is genuinely profitable.

A typical realistic breakeven is **14–22 months** for a mid-traffic shop, longer if you've overpaid for the deposit or the equipment. The single biggest mistake first-time owners make is over-investing in the printer -- a ₹2.5L machine on a ₹1L-a-month revenue shop takes much longer to justify than a ₹80k entry machine.

## Permits needed (Karnataka-specific, verify locally)

Treat this as a starting checklist, not legal advice. Requirements vary by state and change over time.

- **Shop and Establishment Act registration** -- required for most commercial premises in Karnataka through the Labour Department portal.
- **GST registration** -- required if turnover crosses the current threshold (verify with the GST portal; it is revised periodically). Even below the threshold, having GST lets you issue proper invoices to business customers.
- **Trade licence** -- issued by BBMP for Bengaluru, or the local municipal body outside BBMP limits.
- **Udyam (MSME) registration** -- free, online, and useful for future MSME loan eligibility.
- **Police NOC** -- sometimes required for cybercafé-adjacent activities; not always required for plain xerox shops, but confirm locally.
- **Signage permission** -- BBMP signage rules apply in Bengaluru; permits are usually straightforward for shop signage.

For shops outside Karnataka, the equivalent processes are managed by the respective state -- confirm current requirements with a local CA before signing a lease.

## Common mistakes

- **Over-investing in the printer.** A bigger machine doesn't print more pages if you don't have the footfall. Start with what the street supports, upgrade when the volume justifies it.
- **Ignoring the college calendar.** Volume is seasonal. Plan cash flow for the slow months (Dec–Jan, May–Jun) and staff up for the spikes (Aug–Sep, Mar–Apr).
- **Not differentiating.** If your shop is identical to the one next door, only price matters and margins erode. Add something -- colour, binding, photo prints, ID services -- that the next shop doesn't.
- **Skipping the legal setup.** Operating without a GST number means you can't service B2B customers properly. A trade licence is needed for the deposit to be recoverable if you ever need to exit.
- **Setting rates from memory.** Walk the street. Ask. B&W per-side rates in the same area vary by ₹1–₹2; that's the difference between a 50% margin and a 30% margin at the same volume.
