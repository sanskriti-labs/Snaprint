Walk past any print shop near a Bengaluru college campus in the week before submissions and you'll see the same thing: a queue out the door, a shop owner running between the counter and the printer, and at least a few students leaving without their prints because there simply wasn't time.

This isn't a one-off. It happens every semester, on a predictable schedule, and it's a structural problem with how walk-in print shops are built — not a staffing or equipment failure.

## The demand spike is real and predictable

Assignment deadlines and exam schedules aren't random — they cluster. Every student at a college submitting the same coursework needs printing in the same 48–72 hour window, often the same day. A shop that comfortably handles 50 prints an hour on a normal Tuesday needs to handle 5–10x that volume the day before a submission deadline, with the same one counter and one person.

That's not a demand problem. It's a capacity problem that only shows up a few days a semester — which is exactly why most shops never build for it. Hiring extra staff for three predictable rush days a month doesn't make sense on paper, so shops just absorb the queue and lose the customers who can't wait.

## What actually happens during the rush

A few consistent patterns show up near every college gate during exam season:

- **Queues form around the physical bottleneck** — one person taking files, collecting payment, and running the printer simultaneously.
- **WhatsApp orders pile up untracked** — students who can't queue in person send a PDF and say "print 2 copies, I'll collect this evening," and the shop owner tries to remember all of it during the busiest hour of the week.
- **Students walk to the next shop** — not because it's better, but because it's less crowded right now. That's a permanent loss, not a delayed sale; most students don't come back to try again later.
- **Color and binding jobs get deprioritized** — because they take longer per customer, so during a rush, higher-margin work gets pushed aside for faster black-and-white jobs just to clear the queue.

## Why "hire someone for exam week" doesn't solve it

The obvious fix — bring in extra staff during peak weeks — runs into the same problem every seasonal business has: the rush is too short and too irregular to justify hiring, training, and paying someone for a few days a month. Most shop owners either overstaff and eat the cost the rest of the month, or absorb the queue and lose the overflow. Neither is a good trade.

The actual bottleneck isn't people — it's that a single counter can only serve one customer's file at a time, no matter how many hands are behind it.
