Unattended print kiosks are becoming a real category in India — a machine that takes a file from someone's phone, prints it, and takes payment, with nobody standing behind a counter. If you're evaluating whether to put one in your shop, here's what the Snaprint model actually looks like: the investment, what's included, and how it's different from running a kiosk as a standalone box in a mall.

## The core idea: your shop, not a booth

Most people picture a "printing kiosk business" as a free-standing machine you place somewhere and hope people find. Snaprint works differently. The kiosk goes **inside an existing shop** — often a xerox or stationery shop that's already getting footfall — and turns it into a location that can take orders from anyone's phone, any time, whether or not staff are at the counter.

If you already run a xerox or printing shop, this isn't a new business — it's your existing shop capturing revenue it's currently losing. Shop owners already get WhatsApped a file with "print 2 copies, I'll come by later." That's real demand, but it's untracked and easy to lose during a busy hour. A Snaprint kiosk turns that same behaviour into a paid, self-service order the moment it comes in — including outside your normal working hours.

## What you actually keep

There's no per-print commission taken out of every transaction — the only per-order cost is the standard payment merchant fee any UPI transaction carries. Snaprint's own revenue comes from the yearly platform fee, not from skimming each print. That's a deliberate structural choice: the earning potential scales with how much your kiosk actually prints, not with how much Snaprint can take off each order.

Actual monthly earning depends heavily on your specific footfall and the per-page rate at your location — a kiosk near a busy college gate will print very different volumes than one on a quiet side street. As a rough way to think about it: estimate your realistic daily print volume based on your current walk-in and WhatsApp-order pattern, multiply by your per-page rate, and subtract only the payment merchant fee — there's no Snaprint commission to factor in beyond that. Ask during your site visit for a volume estimate specific to your location.

## Reliability — the honest objection

The most common pushback on any unattended machine is "what happens when it breaks or nobody's there to fix a jam." A few things address this directly:

- **You get a live dashboard on your phone** — print counts, revenue, ink and paper levels, all visible without walking to the machine. If paper is low or something needs attention, you know before a customer does.
- **Support is a Bengaluru team, not a call centre.** Snaprint's on-site installation and training happen in person, and support is handled by people who've actually seen the machine, not a ticket queue.
- **After the included 6-month support window**, ongoing technical checks, fault support, and device upkeep (things like periodic cleaning and hardware checks) continue indefinitely under the yearly platform fee — support doesn't have an end date.

Files are encrypted in transit and deleted automatically after each print, so there's no document storage or privacy exposure to manage either.

## Launch timeline

The path from applying to going live follows a straightforward sequence:

1. **Apply** — share your shop location and rough daily print volume.
2. **Site visit** — the Snaprint team visits to check space, power, and internet. Takes about 30 minutes.
3. **Agreement** — a one-page agreement. You own the machine; Snaprint supports the software. No hidden clauses.
4. **Go live** — installation, setup, and training, typically around 10 days from payment.

## Speed, in practice

The dedicated hardware is built for volume: a typical 7-page black & white document prints in **under 60 seconds**, start to finish — fast enough that a small queue at a college gate or office cluster clears quickly instead of building up.
