Every xerox machine price list you find online sorts by brand — Canon vs. Brother vs. Kyocera vs. Epson — as if the brand name is what decides the number. It isn't. Two machines from two different brands in the same duty-cycle class cost roughly the same; two machines from the *same* brand in different classes can be ₹2 lakh apart. ## What actually determines the price, not the badge on the front

Multifunction copiers are priced by **class**, not brand:

- **Entry laser MFP:** built for light, steady counter volume. Print/copy/scan, no finishing extras.
- **Mid-range multifunction copier:** higher monthly duty cycle, faster print speed, better paper handling (multiple trays, larger capacity).
- **Heavy-duty production copier:** built for continuous high-volume runs, the class a busy college-gate or market-street shop actually needs.

## Canon, Brother, Kyocera, Epson: what each is actually known for

Brand positioning, not specific model pricing (get that from a dealer quote):

- **Canon:** strong presence across all three classes, particularly common in the mid-range and heavy-duty production segment for shops that need reliable high-volume output.
- **Kyocera:** known for long-life imaging components (their drum units are built to outlast standard consumables). This matters most for heavy-duty-class buyers running the highest monthly volume.
- **Brother:** well represented in the entry and mid-range classes, a common choice for shops starting lean.
- **Epson:** the outlier. Many of Epson's business machines use ink-tank technology rather than laser/toner, which can undercut laser MFPs on running cost for high-volume B&W and color work. Duty-cycle ratings and paper-handling specs still vary a lot, so check the specific model's rated monthly volume before assuming an ink-tank machine matches a laser copier's throughput.

None of this replaces getting quotes from more than one dealer for the same class. Brand loyalty is a weaker signal than an actual side-by-side quote on comparable specs.

## The one spec that matters more than the brand: duty cycle

The number that determines whether a machine survives your actual shop is its **rated monthly duty cycle**: how many pages per month the manufacturer certifies it to handle without accelerated wear. Every brand publishes this; almost nobody checks it before buying.

Buy under your real volume and the machine burns out early, with repair costs and downtime eating whatever you saved on the sticker price. Buy for peak-day volume when your average day is much lighter, and you've overpaid for headroom you rarely use. Both mistakes are common, and both come from skipping one number on a spec sheet.

If you don't have real volume data yet (most first-time buyers don't), estimate from footfall you can actually observe: walk the street, count competitors' queues at peak times, rather than trusting a dealer's optimistic projection.
